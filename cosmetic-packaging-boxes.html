A clear production boundary

We package the primary pack. We do not make or fill it.

Our scope starts after the bottle, jar, tube, compact, or dispenser has been selected. We translate its dimensions, tolerances, filled weight, decoration, closure, and retail requirements into secondary paper packaging.

  • Folding paper cartons for individual retail units
  • Rigid paper boxes and launch sets for premium presentation
  • Paperboard, molded pulp, or protective inserts for controlled fit
  • Pressure-sensitive labels coordinated with the outer packaging

Outside our scope: bottles, jars, tubes, pumps, caps, formulas, filling, and primary product-contact packaging. The brand remains responsible for formula, container, label-copy, and market compliance approval.

Container-led engineering

Protect the high points, not just the footprint.

A bottle can fit the base dimensions and still fail at the pump, cap, shoulder, label edge, or decorated surface. The pack-out must consider the whole filled unit.

Bottle and jar clearance

Design to controlled maximums

Use production-equivalent units or maximum dimensions, including decoration and closure tolerances. Fit should restrain movement without scraping labels, print, or coatings.

Pump-head protection

Carry load below the actuator

Support the container body or shoulder and preserve headspace around the pump, cap, collar, and locking clip so compression does not trigger or damage the dispenser.

Insert selection

Use only the protection needed

Paperboard, molded pulp, or foam each changes cavity precision, recovery, surface contact, presentation, assembly time, and end-of-life options.

Fit input sheet

Measure what changes the structure.

Nominal capacity is not enough. Packaging dimensions should come from the production container, its closure, its decoration, and the way it will be packed.

Container envelope Maximum width, depth, and height, including decoration, seams, and label edges. Sets cavity size, carton clearance, insert walls, and protection against rubbing.
Cap or pump Actuator height, collar, overcap, locking clip, and any position that changes in use. Defines headspace and where vertical or side loads may safely be carried.

Pack-out Hand or line packing, opening direction, leaflet, accessories, batch code, and tamper label. Changes assembly order, glue areas, insert access, and the space available for required copy.

Handling and surface choice

Plan for fingers, oil, rubbing, and the packing line.

Matte, soft-touch, uncoated, film-laminated, and varnished surfaces behave differently during filling, packing, retail handling, and transport. The best finish is the one that still looks intentional after real contact.

  • Review dark solids and matte finishes for fingerprints, edge rub, and visible scuffs
  • Keep exposed container oil or residue away from unprotected paper and glue areas
  • Test foil, embossing, and coatings across scores, folds, and high-contact panels
  • Use the filled production pack during the physical sample and handling review

Box and label coordination

One color target. Two different surfaces.

Boxboard and label stock do not reflect ink in the same way. A coated carton, uncoated paper label, white film, and clear film can all shift the same digital color differently.

  • Start from one approved master artwork and named color reference
  • Record substrate, white point, coating, opacity, and print process for each item
  • Review box and label proofs side by side under the same lighting
  • Approve a visual match range instead of assuming unlike materials will look identical
