How to verify an FSC-certified packaging supplier.

Match the public certificate record to the direct supplier, production scope, actual order, sales claim, label approval, and retained evidence.

By GloryStarPack Packaging Team

SearchCurrent public record
MatchHolder + site + scope
OrderClaim + label + documents

Short answer

Verify the supplier and the transaction—not only the PDF.

Ask the direct packaging supplier for its legal name, FSC certificate code, FSC licence code, certified site, and the claim proposed for your finished packaging. Check those details in the FSC Public Search. Then confirm that the actual packaging item, material route, production activity, sales documents, and on-product label are handled within the applicable certified scope.

A public record can confirm the organization and certification information shown in the database. It does not prove that every item the company sells is certified or that your specific order has been entered, produced, claimed, labelled, and delivered as an FSC-certified product.

Pause on mismatch. If the supplier name, manufacturing site, code, status, product information, or proposed claim does not align, do not publish an FSC claim or approve label artwork until the direct supplier and its certification body reconcile the evidence.

Evidence map

Know what each record can prove.

A certificate, website logo, quotation, invoice, label artwork, and physical package answer different questions. Keep them connected to one order revision.

Step 1 · Identify the seller

Start with the organization taking the order.

Record the legal entity that will quote, contract, invoice, and supply the finished packaging. A sales contact, trading name, marketplace profile, parent company, paper mill, or subcontract printer may not be the certificate holder that controls the certified transaction.

  • Ask for the legal company name in English and the local registered language where applicable
  • Record the contracting address, manufacturing site, certificate code, licence code, and certification body
  • Clarify whether the supplier is a single site, member of a group, or covered through a multi-site certificate
  • Identify any broker, converter, subcontractor, printer, assembler, or fulfilment party that touches the order

If the supplier and manufacturer are different entities, ask who owns the certified sale, production control, label approval, and delivery documentation.

Step 2 · Check the database

Match more than the company name.

Search the FSC public database by legal name and both codes. Open the detailed record instead of accepting a screenshot of the results page. Save the registry URL and the date checked in the procurement file.

  • Status and certification type shown for the record
  • Exact holder name, country, address, and listed sites or members relevant to production
  • Certificate code and trademark licence code matching the supplied evidence
  • Product classifications and activities that reasonably cover the proposed packaging operation
  • Any suspension, termination, scope change, or other status that affects reliance on the claim

Similar company names are not interchangeable. A certificate held by a parent, sister company, paper merchant, or different plant should not be extended to the direct supplier without documented coverage.

Step 3 · Control the order

Turn a general certificate into item-level evidence.

A certified company can also sell non-certified products. Your purchase file should identify which route is being ordered and which claim is expected on the transaction.

  • RFQName the finished packaging item, material route, quantity, destination, required claim or label objective, and documents needed at each stage.
  • QuoteRequire the supplier to identify the proposed certified item, site, claim, codes, material assumptions, label service, exclusions, and price basis.
  • Purchase orderCarry the approved item code, specification revision, claim, artwork revision, supplier identity, evidence requirements, and change-approval rule.
  • ProductionPrevent unapproved substitutions of paper, board, mill source, site, subcontractor, structure, or artwork that could affect scope or label use.
  • DeliveryReconcile item, quantity, claim, supplier codes, packing record, and delivered label against the approved purchase file.

Step 4 · Approve the label

Do not build the FSC mark from an online image.

The packaging artwork route and the organization's promotional use are not the same permission. Define who is responsible before the designer releases files.

Supplier

Generate the correct label

FSC states that certified suppliers have access to the official label graphics. The supplier should select the applicable label route and manage approval through its certification system.

Designer

Protect the approved artwork

Use the supplied approved asset without redrawing, changing the licence code, compressing proportions, removing elements, or moving it outside the approved layout.

Brand

Separate product and promotion

Confirm whether website, catalogue, campaign, or corporate promotion by a non-certified organization needs a promotional licence or separate approval.

Prepress

Lock the release version

Keep the final label approval with the production PDF, editable artwork, dieline, item code, date, and written approval trail.

Control label assets, finishing layers, proofs, and production release in the packaging artwork checklist

Red flags

Stop the claim when the evidence changes shape.

A mismatch is a request for clarification, not proof of fraud. Pause the certified route until the responsible parties reconcile it.

Monitoring

Recheck at decisions, not only at audits.

Build certificate control into supplier onboarding and item change management. A calendar reminder alone will not catch a new factory, broker, subcontractor, board route, artwork version, or suspended record introduced between annual reviews.

  • At onboarding: record the public URL, codes, holder, sites, scope, certification body, and responsible buyer
  • At quotation: check current status and confirm the exact certified item, claim, label, and supplying entity
  • Before production: approve specification, site, material route, artwork, label approval, and substitution controls
  • At delivery: reconcile sales documents, quantity, item code, claim, codes, physical label, and packing evidence
  • For repeat orders: recheck after certificate, site, product, supplier, material, claim, or artwork changes

Assign ownership to procurement or quality rather than waiting for a retailer or customer audit to discover that the stored evidence is stale.

Claim boundaries

Do not make one certificate answer every sustainability question.

FSC chain of custody concerns forest-based material and certified supply-chain control. Other packaging claims need their own evidence.

Not automatic

Every product or component

A holder may supply certified and non-certified outputs. Confirm the actual item, forest-based components, claim, and certified transaction.

Separate evidence

Recycled content and recyclability

Material percentage, coatings, adhesives, mixed components, collection, sorting, and destination systems require claim-specific support.

Separate evidence

Product safety and compliance

Food contact, chemicals, migration, labeling law, destination requirements, and product protection are not replaced by a chain-of-custody certificate.

Separate evidence

Carbon and total impact

Weight, conversion, waste, transport, reuse, disposal, and carbon calculations need defined boundaries and suitable data.

Review material reduction, separation, inserts, finishing, and responsible claim boundaries

Community signal

Why certificate tracking belongs in the buying workflow.

A recent Reddit packaging and procurement discussion began with a supplier certificate that did not match the public registry. Replies raised the operational issue: many teams store a PDF or spreadsheet, forget to refresh it, and only discover the gap when a retailer or auditor asks for proof. The discussion is useful as a current buyer-language signal, not as certification authority.

The practical response is to connect supplier identity, public status, product scope, transaction claim, artwork approval, and delivery evidence to one owned process. Community comments and vendor statements should not override the current FSC database, applicable standards, or guidance from the relevant certification body.

Official references

Use FSC sources for certification and trademark decisions.

Use the FSC Public Search to inspect certificate records. FSC's chain-of-custody overview explains certified material identification and sales-document claims. Its on-product label guidance explains the certified supplier's role in label access and approval, while the trademark-use page links the mandatory trademark standard and approval guidance.

This page is procurement guidance, not certification, trademark, legal, environmental-claim, or regulatory advice. Confirm the current requirements with FSC, the relevant certification body, and qualified advisers for the actual organization, product, market, and communication.
