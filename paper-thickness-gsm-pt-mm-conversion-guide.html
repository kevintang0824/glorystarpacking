Convert paper thickness: GSM, pt and mm.

Short answer

PT, mm, microns, and inches describe thickness. GSM does not.

Points, millimetres, micrometres, and inches are dimensional units, so they can be converted mathematically. In this guide, “pt” means the paper and packaging caliper point of 0.001 inch—not the typographic point used for font size. One caliper point is exactly 0.0254 mm or 25.4 micrometres. GSM means grams per square metre: it describes mass per area, not physical thickness.

That distinction matters in packaging. Two boards with the same GSM can have different density, fiber composition, coating, bulk, moisture, stiffness, and caliper. Two boards with the same caliper can also crease, print, wrap, and carry load differently. Use the converter for unit translation, then specify the real board family, grade, caliper tolerance, surface, structure, and sample acceptance.

Three different specifications

Do not collapse mass, thickness, and performance into one number.

Each value answers a different procurement question. A reliable quotation keeps all three attached to the named material and finished structure.

01 · Mass

GSM is grams per square metre

GSM describes how much a square metre of material weighs. It can support purchasing and yield calculations, but it does not state the board's thickness, density, bending stiffness, coating, or crease performance.

02 · Dimension

Caliper is physical thickness

Caliper may be written in pt, mm, micrometres, or inches. It affects panel buildup, cavity size, scores, wrapped corners, lid fit, drawer clearance, and outside dimensions—but still does not describe the complete material.

03 · Use

Performance belongs to the converted pack

Stiffness, fold quality, load response, surface rub, moisture behavior, packing-line performance, and transit protection depend on the actual grade, grain, construction, finish, geometry, manufacturing condition, and test method.

Choose by packaging family

Thickness enters each structure differently.

Folding cartons
Caliper works with grade, grain, crease geometry, coating, panel span, closure, product load, and packing line. Review the complete folding carton specification.
Rigid boxes
Board thickness changes corner buildup, lid fit, drawer clearance, insert cavity, wrap geometry, weight, and shipping cube. Start with the custom rigid box route.
Inserts
Paperboard caliper affects fold, cavity depth, retention, edges, and stacked buildup; foam or molded fiber needs density and geometry rather than a paper conversion. Compare packaging insert structures.
Corrugated
Total caliper depends on the combined board and manufacturing condition. Flute, liners, medium, scores, load and agreed tests belong in the shipping-box specification.

Measurement workflow

Define where, when, and how caliper is measured.

A bare incoming sheet, printed and laminated sheet, wrapped board, scored blank, and finished corner are different measurement objects. Name the object in the specification.

  • Identify the board family, supplier proposal, finish, and production state
  • Agree the nominal caliper, tolerance, instrument, contact area, and pressure
  • Condition materials when moisture or climate can change the result
  • Measure representative areas away from crushed scores, edges, and local defects
  • Record readings with the material lot, sample revision, and approved construction
  • Approve fold, fit, load, print, finish, packing, and transit—not caliper alone
