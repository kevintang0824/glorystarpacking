Define the target

“Waterproof” starts with a condition and a clock.

Brief contact with droplets, repeated condensation, timed immersion, freeze-thaw, oily handling, rubbing, and outdoor exposure are different tests. State the sequence, duration, temperature, and acceptable result.

EX–01

Water + condensation

Record droplet source, temperature cycle, contact time, drying, repetition, and whether the label may be rubbed while wet.

  • Splash
  • Condensation
  • Wet rub
EX–02

Immersion + freezing

Define liquid, depth, time, temperature, thaw sequence, cycles, and what must remain attached, legible, and scannable.

  • Timed immersion
  • Freeze-thaw
  • Dry recovery
EX–03

Oil + rubbing + outdoors

Name the oil, contact method, rubbing action, outdoor duration, orientation, and inspection intervals instead of assuming one universal result.

  • Oil contact
  • Dry abrasion
  • Outdoor interval

The complete construction

Match face, print protection, and adhesive to one brief.

The face material carries the print, the adhesive must wet out on the actual application surface, and a laminate or coating can protect the printed face. These layers need to be assessed together under the intended application and exposure sequence.

  • Identify surface material, texture, curvature, coating, and surface-energy challenges
  • Record application temperature, pressure, and dwell before the first exposure
  • Define whether the print faces water, oil, rubbing, or outdoor exposure
  • Check edge lift, wrinkling, print change, adhesion, readability, and barcode scans

A face material, adhesive, or protective layer cannot establish performance alone. Approve the complete applied construction against a written test plan.

Exposure and test matrix

Turn use conditions into repeatable checks.

The useful question is not whether a label is waterproof in general, but whether the applied construction meets the stated acceptance rule after each representative condition.

Example inputs to define before material selection and representative testing.
Exposure Define before testing Inspect or measure
Water contact Water source, droplet or spray method, volume, contact time, angle, repetition, and dry interval. Edge lift, face change, print change, wrinkling, adhesion, and readability.
Condensation Starting temperature, room temperature and humidity, cycle length, number of cycles, and handling while wet. Moisture at edges, bubbles, movement, whitening, print condition, and wet-rub response.
Immersion Liquid, depth, temperature, duration, agitation if any, position, removal method, and recovery time. Attachment, edge condition, face distortion, print, readability, and post-dry appearance.
Freezing Application dwell before cooling, freezing temperature, hold time, thaw conditions, cycle count, and moisture present. Lift, cracking, wrinkles, movement, print condition, adhesion after thaw, and scan result.
Oil contact Named oil, amount, contact area, temperature, duration, wipe method, repetition, and recovery interval. Staining, softening, smearing, edge lift, adhesion, readability, and surface change.
Rubbing Wet or dry state, rubbing material, load, stroke length, speed, cycle count, and contact area. Ink loss, laminate damage, scuffing, tearing, code contrast, and remaining legibility.
Outdoor exposure Location, orientation, season, duration, inspection intervals, direct or sheltered position, and expected weather. Color shift, print contrast, face change, edge lift, adhesion, cracking, and barcode readability.
Barcode Code type, size, data, quiet zone, print method, scanner, scan distance, and acceptance rule. Baseline scan plus scans after each exposure, with visible defects and failures recorded.

Application surface and dwell

A strong test begins before the first drop of water.

Surface energy, texture, contamination, curve, application temperature, pressure, and dwell influence how the adhesive develops contact. A test that ignores application conditions cannot represent the intended use.

  • Use the actual surfaceTest representative containers, equipment, totes, or cartons with the intended coating, texture, and curvature.
  • Control preparationDocument cleaning, drying, handling, application temperature, and application pressure.
  • Set the dwellDefine how long the label remains undisturbed before water, cold, oil, rubbing, or outdoor exposure begins.
  • Record the sequenceRun exposures in the same order expected in use and retain an unexposed comparison sample.

For cold beverage bottles, use the wine label condensation and adhesive test guide. For the secondary pack, pair the label brief with custom corrugated shipping boxes.

From exposure brief to release

Approve against evidence from the planned test.

A useful development path fixes the target conditions, representative samples, observation points, and acceptance rules before production.

01 · Define

Write the exposure

List the surface, water, condensation, immersion, freezing, oil, rubbing, outdoor, and barcode conditions that apply.

02 · Construct

Plan the label system

Review face material, adhesive, print protection, shape, application temperature, dwell, and supply format together.

03 · Test

Use representative samples

Apply under recorded conditions, run the agreed sequence, scan codes, inspect defects, and compare with the acceptance rules.

04 · Release

Fix the approved specification

Sign the artwork, construction, roll details, test record, QC points, packing, quantity, and delivery plan before scale.
