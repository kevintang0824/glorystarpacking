Wet strength and condensation

Design for the cold bottle, not only the dry proof.

Moisture can darken paper, lift edges, soften fibers, or expose adhesive haze. The risk depends on the face stock, adhesive, varnish or laminate, bottle coating, label shape, application conditions, and time between labeling and use.

  • Identify refrigeration, ice-bucket, wash-off, humidity, and abrasion exposure
  • Apply trial labels to representative clean bottles—not only flat test panels
  • Condition samples through the expected cold-to-room-temperature cycle
  • Review edge lift, wrinkling, color change, scuffing, and removal behavior

A material described as “water resistant” is not automatically suitable for every cold, wet, coated, or curved bottle. Approve the complete label construction on the intended container.

Production specification

Give the printer the same brief as the applicator.

Artwork is only one part of a usable label order. Container data and converting requirements determine whether finished rolls can run cleanly on your line.

Confirm these decisions before the physical proof or production order.

Bottle Material, coating, diameter or taper, application panel, and a representative bottle sample. Adhesion, edge lift, wrinkles, label alignment, and visual show-through.
Environment Application temperature, service temperature, refrigeration, condensation, ice bucket, and humidity exposure. Wet strength, adhesive hold, ink protection, scuff resistance, and appearance after conditioning.

Foil and emboss registration

Make one detail tactile and precise.

Registered foil and embossing can combine reflectivity with relief, but the artwork must leave room for the paper, foil carrier, dies, pressure, and normal converting movement to work together.

  • Protect fine detailReview thin rules, small counters, reverse type, and tightly spaced letterforms before tooling.
  • Match the stockDeeply textured papers can change foil coverage and the apparent sharpness of an embossed edge.
  • Define the hierarchyUse foil and relief on a focal mark rather than making every element compete for attention.
  • Approve physicallyJudge registration, pressure, cracking, pinholes, and readability on the intended material.

Buyer questions

Custom wine label FAQ.

Final material, production, and timing recommendations depend on the actual bottle, artwork, quantity, and use conditions.

Share the finished label size, quantity, bottle material and shape, application method, expected moisture or cold exposure, artwork, finishing direction, delivery country, and any roll specifications from the label applicator.
Wet-strength paper or film should be reviewed when bottles may face refrigeration, condensation, ice buckets, damp storage, or repeated handling. The final construction still needs to be checked on the actual bottle and under the intended conditions.
Foil and embossing can be designed as a registered combination, but line weight, paper texture, die geometry, and production tolerance affect the result. Artwork and a physical proof should be reviewed before bulk approval.
Use the applicator manufacturer's winding diagram and provide the required unwind direction, core inside diameter, maximum roll outside diameter, web width, and gap between labels. There is no single roll specification that suits every application line.
Adhesive selection starts with the bottle material, surface coating, curvature, label size, application temperature, service temperature, moisture exposure, and expected shelf life. Testing on representative filled bottles is recommended before production.
