Test selection

“Tested” is incomplete without a method, sample, and decision.

Packaging can be exposed to opening cycles, friction, moisture, oils, temperature, stacking, vibration, compression, drops, handling, or a product-specific chemical environment. Select the test from the failure that matters to the product and route—not from a generic list of claims.

Boundary: this guide defines what to record. It does not set a universal pass/fail limit, create a certification, choose an ASTM or ISO method, or claim that any GloryStarPack project has passed a test without its project record.

1 · Map the failure mode

Start with the decision the test needs to support.

The same packaging can need different tests when the product, surface, climate, application, route, or customer expectation changes.

2 · Build the record

Retain enough detail for another qualified person to reproduce the decision.

  • Identify the sampleProduct, packaging configuration, material, artwork, revision, lot or sample ID, quantity, and whether the sample represents production.
  • State the methodNamed method or internal procedure, equipment, settings, sequence, preparation, conditioning, operator, and deviations.
  • Capture the actual resultMeasurements, observations, cycle count, exposure, photos, defect location, failure mode, and the condition after the test.
  • Record the decisionPass, fail, conditional, more evidence required, redesign, retest, or another defined disposition with an owner and date.
  • Link the correctionWhen the result changes the material, artwork, structure, packing, or method, create a new revision and identify the retest trigger.

Use the e-commerce mailer transit guide for a route-specific example, and the waterproof label testing guide for exposure planning.

3 · Place the test in the workflow

Test before the next irreversible decision.

A test that happens after artwork release, bulk conversion, or shipment may document a problem without preventing it.

  1. 01

    Define the intended use

    Describe the product, user, application, exposure, handling, storage, transport, destination, and failure consequence.

  2. 02

    Choose a representative sample

    Match the material build, finish, adhesive, insert, closure, product mass, artwork, and pack-out to the decision being made.

  3. 03

    Approve the method and limits

    Name the current method, conditions, sample count, measurements, rating, acceptance criteria, and decision owner before testing.

  4. 04

    Record and review

    Keep the full observation record, photos, deviations, failures, correction, and re-test status with the sample revision.

Buyer questions

Packaging test records FAQ.

Project-specific methods and acceptance limits must be selected by the qualified owner for the application.

Record the purpose and failure mode, sample identity and revision, method, equipment, conditioning, load or exposure, sequence, observations, measurements, photographs, deviations, result, decision owner, and any corrective action or retest.
There is no universal test panel. Select tests from the product, packaging structure, materials, surface, application, handling, environment, transport, destination, and contractual or regulatory requirements.
No. A test record describes one method and result for an identified sample. Certification or regulatory status requires the applicable authority, scope, current document, product and material identity, and any required review.
Test when the result can change the design or release decision: during structure development, on a representative sample, after material or artwork changes, before production release, and after a relevant process or packing change.
