Bottle data before dieline

Control every projection, finish, and weight-bearing surface.

Fragrance bottles often combine heavy glass, irregular shoulders, fitted caps, spray components, labels, coatings, and decorated faces. A product name or nominal volume is not enough to define the box.

Base support and top clearance

Carry the bottle below; keep the spray assembly clear above.

The insert and box should manage the bottle's filled weight through approved structural zones. The pump, spray head, collar, cap, label, and decorated glass need documented clearance rather than accidental contact.

  • Support the base or lower body without loading an unstable punt, decorative foot, label edge, or fragile projection
  • Use approved body or shoulder zones to locate the bottle while allowing for glass and assembly variation
  • Define a no-contact envelope around the spray head, pump, collar, cap, and any loosely fitted decorative component
  • Check lid, tuck flap, accessory tray, insert movement, and stacking pressure against that top envelope
  • Inspect closing, handling, opening, removal, and replacement with the filled production-equivalent item

Surface protection and removal

Hold the bottle where the customer can still lift it cleanly.

A tight cavity can create rub, finger pressure, cap pulling, or an awkward reveal. Retention and removal should be reviewed as one handling sequence.

  • Contact map Mark glass, coatings, labels, printed decoration, metallized surfaces, high-gloss faces, cap finishes, and seams as permitted, conditional, or no-contact zones.
  • Edge isolation Cover or position cut edges, joints, hard cores, adhesive areas, and accessory components so they cannot reach visible bottle surfaces during transit or removal.
  • Movement control Use the base, approved body or shoulder support, side pads, platforms, or separate cavities to limit movement without compressing the pump or cap.
  • Removal access Add finger recesses, side access, lift ribbons, removable platforms, or a controlled tray so the user lifts from the bottle body rather than the cap or spray assembly.
  • Material review Sample proposed paper, board, fiber, foam, fabric, flock, adhesive, and seams with the actual surface for marks, abrasion, fiber transfer, odor, fit, and repeated removal.

Folding carton versus presentation box

Choose the structure from the bottle, channel, and opening.

Folding cartons and rigid formats solve different assembly, presentation, storage, and pack-out needs. Neither route removes the need for confirmed bottle clearance and loaded sampling.

Compact secondary pack

Folding carton

A folding carton can use flaps, paperboard partitions, or a separate insert around the bottle. Review board behavior under the filled weight, bottom and top closure, assembly, insert loading, cap clearance, and how the bottle is removed.

Structured presentation

Rigid or magnetic box

A custom rigid box supports a wider reveal and fitted interior; a magnetic box adds a hinged close. Specify loaded base behavior, insert seating, hinge and lid clearance, closure effort, and surface contact.

Round silhouette

Paper tube route

Custom paper tube packaging can suit selected cylindrical or compact bottle presentations. Confirm usable inside diameter, bottle anti-rotation, base support, cap and lid clearance, removal access, and stable outer-carton packing.

Box, label, and color coordination

Build one visual family with separate production controls.

A perfume range may coordinate bottle labels, folding cartons, rigid gift boxes, inserts, sample sizes, and set packaging. Shared references help the family look intentional, but each substrate, print route, finish, and viewing condition needs its own approved target.

  • Define master artwork rules for hierarchy, logo size, typography, finish zones, panel order, and required SKU identification
  • Set physical color references for the proposed box, label, insert, and wrap rather than treating one screen value as a universal result
  • Record which finishes must align across panels and which are independently positioned on separate components
  • Keep separate dielines, artwork files, material references, insert versions, and approval samples for every SKU or set format
  • Review the complete packed family under the brand's intended display and photography conditions

Outer shipping and carton packing

The presentation box is secondary packaging, not automatically a shipper.

Heavy glass, projecting caps, delicate finishes, mixed SKU sets, and presentation surfaces all affect the outer pack. Develop the corrugated shipping box around representative loaded units.

  • Specify unit protection and dividers where box-to-box contact could mark wrap, print, foil, corners, lids, or closures
  • Choose loading orientation from bottle mass, cap clearance, insert retention, presentation-box structure, and opening direction
  • Control units per carton, layer pattern, void management, mixed-SKU rules, gross weight, dimensions, carton identification, and pallet needs if any
  • Review the complete representative pack for the intended handling and distribution route rather than making a universal transit claim

Provide the filled bottle's maximum length, width, height, and weight; base and shoulder geometry; spray pump, collar, and cap dimensions; dimensional variation; label or decorated areas; sensitive surfaces; and intended packed orientation. Clearance depends on the maximum assembled height, cap fit and variation, spray head and collar profile, packed orientation, lid or tuck path, and insert movement. Record the no-contact envelope above and around these components, then inspect a loaded sample through closing, handling, opening, and replacement rather than applying a generic allowance.
