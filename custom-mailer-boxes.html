Specification before artwork

Four decisions shape the whole mailer.

The right structure depends on what goes inside, how it is packed, where it travels, and what the customer should experience when it opens.

01 · Flute

Choose profile by load and span

E-flute keeps compact packs slim with a refined print surface. B-flute adds depth for heavier contents, longer spans, or routes that need more cushioning.

02 · Print

Match process to the artwork

Bold spot-color graphics, fine typography, photographic art, kraft stock, and interior print each point to a different print and liner strategy.

03 · Closure

Secure the outbound trip

Roll-end locks, tuck fronts, seal strips, and tear-open features must work with the chosen board, finish, pack-out speed, and return plan.

Start with the packed product. Internal dimensions, weight distribution, and movement control should be set before exterior dimensions and artwork are finalized.

Procurement guide

Choose what the route requires.

These are starting points, not automatic specifications. Packed weight, exact dimensions, artwork, and a physical sample determine the final construction.

Mailer box structure and print decision guide
Decision Best considered for Procurement check
E-flute Compact DTC packs, fine folds, tighter storage, and a smoother face for detailed graphics. Confirm the profile supports the packed weight and panel span without bowing or corner collapse.
B-flute Heavier products, deeper mailers, stacking exposure, and routes where extra cushioning is useful. Allow for thicker edges, a larger folded footprint, and changes to score and locking geometry.
Direct print Clear logos, bold color fields, handling marks, and artwork suited to the board surface. Review small type, screens, and color expectations on the actual liner—not only on a monitor.
Printed liner Fine illustration, broader color range, photographic detail, or a more controlled retail surface. Approve the laminated sample after scoring and folding because added layers affect creases and locks.
Inside print Opening instructions, campaign messaging, cross-sell content, or a deliberate unboxing sequence. Keep key copy away from folds, locking tabs, inserts, and areas hidden by the product.

Transit engineering

The parcel route belongs in the brief.

A beautiful mailer still fails if the product can move, the lid opens under pressure, or corners collapse in a mixed courier network. The pack-out and route set the performance target.

  • Control empty space with a fitted insert, paper support, or a defined void-fill plan
  • Keep concentrated weight away from unsupported spans and vulnerable score lines
  • Review closure retention after the box is filled, stacked, and handled
  • Check printed surfaces for rub, moisture exposure, labels, and shipping marks

Build the product-fit and parcel-test brief or compare outer shipping carton requirements.

Flat-pack economics

Save space without slowing the packing bench.

Roll-end mailers normally arrive flat, reducing empty-box storage and inbound volume. The useful saving comes only when staff can open, lock, fill, and close the box consistently.

  • Set score direction and depth for repeatable folding without cracked print
  • Balance secure locks against unnecessary force or assembly steps
  • Confirm flat dimensions against shelves, cartons, pallets, and packing stations
  • Use a pack-out trial to measure assembly flow before a large production run

Sample to production

Approve the structure in the right order.

Fit and folding come first. Print and finish are approved on the structure that will actually be packed and shipped.

01 · Brief

Pack-out data

Product size, packed weight, quantity, route, packing method, closure, artwork, and target date.

02 · Structure

White sample

Check internal fit, locks, folding order, assembly effort, insert, and product movement.

03 · Prepress

Printed proof

Review color, registration, key copy, barcodes, finish, scores, and the open-box sequence.

04 · Production

QC + packing

Inspect dimensions, print, glue, scoring, locks, count, flat packing, and export carton protection.
