Selection logic

Start with the constraints the box must resolve.

A useful structure brief explains the product and the buying moment, then defines what the box must do from packing line to final opening.

  • Product dimensions, weight, fragile areas, and required orientation
  • Opening sequence, product visibility, removal method, and reuse expectation
  • Manual or line packing, storage space, master-carton limits, and destination
  • Artwork hierarchy, finish priorities, quantity, target date, and revision allowance

Material system

Board, wrap, and finish must work together.

Material selection follows the structure, product load, color target, surface treatment, and handling conditions.

Core board

Thickness follows the structure

Board thickness is reviewed against box span, lid depth, product weight, insert support, and the intended opening feel.

Wrap paper

Printability and touch

Coated, uncoated, textured, and specialty papers behave differently under color, lamination, foil, embossing, and folding.

Surface finish

One clear focal treatment

Foil, embossing, debossing, spot gloss, and lamination are selected around artwork scale, registration, handling, and budget.

Material references

Specify the surface in the sample.

Screen color and reference photography are useful direction, but the physical sample confirms texture, sheen, color interaction, and edge treatment.

Textured paper

Visible grain and a quieter printed surface

Uncoated stock

Soft touch with specific print considerations

Dark specialty stock

Opaque base for foil, embossing, or selected inks

Foil detail

Reflective focus with artwork and registration limits

Custom inserts

The insert defines fit, reveal, and removal.

An insert should hold the product during transit without making unpacking difficult. Material choice follows the product, structure, presentation goal, and disposal requirements.

  • Die-cut paperboard platforms for clean presentation and material simplicity
  • Molded fiber concepts where geometry and project requirements allow
  • Foam or covered foam when cushioning and precise cavities are required
  • Pull ribbons, finger notches, documentation wells, and accessory compartments

Quality checkpoints

Define what will be checked before production starts.

Inspection criteria and tolerances depend on the chosen construction and materials. They should be documented in the project specification rather than assumed from a rendering.

  • Finished dimensions, squareness, lid fit, opening movement, and insert retention
  • Artwork position, color reference, foil or emboss registration, and visible surfaces
  • Wrap alignment, corners, edges, seams, glue areas, and assembly consistency
  • Product pack-out, protective packing, master-carton details, and agreed shipment marks
