Selection logic

Specify the close by feel and function.

A strong snap is not automatically a better closure. The right configuration should close reliably, align cleanly, avoid unnecessary material, and suit the product inside.

  • Lid span, hinge direction, overlap, opening angle, and intended one- or two-hand use
  • Product weight, insert support, removal path, accessories, and documentation
  • Closure position, number of contact points, alignment, polarity, and acceptable feel
  • Assembled or collapsible format, packing labor, storage, master cartons, and delivery

Construction layers

Every layer changes the finished close.

Board, wrap, hinge, closure pieces, and insert are reviewed as one assembly because small changes can affect alignment and opening behavior.

Board + hinge

Control the moving geometry

Board thickness, lid proportions, spine construction, wrap direction, and hinge clearance influence how the lid opens and rests.

Closure points

Position before decoration

Magnets and corresponding metal pieces are concealed in planned locations, then checked for polarity, alignment, and closure feel.

Wrap + finish

Protect registration areas

Artwork, foil, embossing, and seams must account for edges, folds, concealed components, and normal production tolerances.

Material decisions

Choose materials for the full assembly.

The physical sample confirms how the surface, edges, hinge, closure, and insert behave together under normal handling.

Textured wrap

Grain direction and fold behavior require review

Dark stock

Surface for selected inks, foil, or blind detail

Embossed detail

Depth and placement tested around board and wrap

Foil accent

Artwork scale and registration confirmed in proofing

Insert engineering

The product should not compete with the closure.

The insert carries the product load and controls its presentation. It should also leave practical access for removal and keep critical product areas away from unintended pressure.

  • Product cavity, orientation, clearance, retention, and finger access
  • Paperboard, molded fiber, foam, or covered insert based on project needs
  • Accessory wells, documentation, charging items, ribbons, and removal sequence
  • Pack-out trial to review movement, surface contact, and closure performance

Quality checkpoints

Inspect the closure as a repeated interaction.

Acceptance criteria depend on the approved construction and materials. The project specification should state what is checked and which physical reference governs the run.

  • Finished dimensions, lid alignment, hinge movement, opening angle, and board condition
  • Closure position, polarity, contact, repeatable closing action, and visible read-through
  • Wrap registration, edges, corners, seams, surface finish, and glue cleanliness
  • Insert fit, product movement, pack-out sequence, protective packing, and carton details

A collapsible magnetic construction may be possible when the dimensions, product load, assembly method, appearance, and quantity support it. The tradeoffs should be reviewed against a permanently assembled box.
Closure feel depends on lid span, board and wrap layers, magnet position, alignment, polarity, and the number and size of closure points. It is evaluated in a structural sample rather than selected from appearance alone.
