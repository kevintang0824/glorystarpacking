Stock and format directions

Choose the tag as a handled component, not a miniature poster.

The sheet must carry the artwork and finishing, hold around the hole or slot, hang at the intended angle, and remain readable after assembly, transport, and retail handling.

HT–01

Single-stock hang tag

A direct route for branding, product information, and attachment on one tag. Stock stiffness, hole position, print, foil, and product scale should be reviewed together.

Tactile and uncoated direction

Useful when surface feel and restrained graphics lead the design. Fiber direction, ink holdout, foil coverage, emboss definition, edge appearance, and rub should be sampled on the selected stock.

  • Material reference
  • Foil trial

Coated or laminated direction

A route for smooth color, surface protection, or a controlled tactile effect. Fold, edge, scratch, blocking, foil adhesion, and write-on requirements can influence the protection choice.

A tag can match the artwork and still fail at the product if its proportions, grain, hole clearance, string length, copy direction, or packing orientation are undefined.

Hole, eyelet, string, and ribbon

Treat the attachment as a small assembly.

The attachment changes the tag's angle, reach, loading method, contact with the product, and packing footprint. A visually suitable cord can still be awkward to thread, too short for the fixture, abrasive against a finish, or difficult to keep untangled.

  • Locate from fixed edgesDimension the hole or slot center from two finished edges and record the tag face, rather than positioning it by eye from the artwork.
  • Define every componentList eyelet, cord, ribbon, fastener, knot, seal, length, color, loop direction, and whether the assembly is fitted before delivery.
  • Trial the real attachment pointCheck threading access, reach, tag angle, product contact, removal goal, and interference with a closure, seam, handle, or display fixture.
  • Pack for applicationChoose bundle direction, separators, tail placement, and counts that reduce tangling, bending, surface rub, and SKU mixing at the packing line.

An eyelet can reinforce and decorate the hole, but the tag stock, eyelet setting, hole geometry, attachment load, and edge clearance still need to be approved as one construction.

Barcode, variable data, and durability

Protect the information that must survive the journey.

Static branding, variable retail data, finishing, and surface protection compete for limited space. Define the scan and handling workflow before placing decorative effects around critical information.

Sample path and quality control

Approve the tag in the same state it will be received.

The approval record should connect artwork, materials, data, components, assembly, product fit, and packing. Each proof must be clear about what it can and cannot establish.

01 · Technical proof

Lock content and geometry

Confirm dieline, sides, orientation, hole center, artwork, print layers, foil, emboss, variable fields, barcode rules, and required finish clearances.

02 · Physical sample

Judge material and attachment

Review stock, size, stiffness, color, print, foil or relief, cut edge, hole or eyelet, string or ribbon, assembly direction, and product fit.

03 · Workflow trial

Scan, handle, and kit

Run representative variable records, scan with the intended device, apply tags to the product, perform the agreed durability checks, and trial the packing sequence.

04 · Production QC

Inspect and reconcile

Check against agreed criteria for content, color, finish, dimensions, attachment, record matching, scan result, assembly, bundle count, SKU identity, and cartons.

If tags are supplied in SKU kits, the accepted sample should show the exact component order, facing direction, attachment state, bundle method, identification, and count used for production packing.
